Communications and account leadership, Cairo

The work usually starts in the same place. An organisation has something worth saying and no settled way of saying it. Sometimes the audience has never really been decided. Sometimes it has been decided by four people who quietly disagree. What I do is work out what is actually being asked for, then organise the people, decisions and deadlines it takes to deliver it.

Position

I'm Senior Account Manager at Dlight Media Solutions in Cairo. The agency's live client portfolio sits with me, which runs across construction, real estate, consumer electronics, mobility, education, healthcare and food.

I've also run The Digital Venture since 2021. It's small on purpose. Instead of carrying a permanent team I build one per assignment from partner agencies, production houses and specialists, so the practice can take on a two-week shoot and a six-month communications programme without pretending they need the same people.

Before that I covered communications and visibility at GIZ Egypt for an EU and BMZ co-funded urban infrastructure programme. For seven years I also ran PR and communications for a Cairo cultural organisation working with NGOs, embassies, government bodies and cultural institutions.

How I work

People ask about process, so here it is without the diagram.

  • I start with the context rather than the brief. A brief tells you what someone wants made. It doesn't usually tell you what the thing is meant to fix, who has to approve it, or what happens if it slips.
  • From there it becomes an account. Priorities, workstreams, who owns what, where decisions actually get taken, and some agreement on how we'll know whether it worked.
  • Alignment is the stage people underestimate. Client, strategy, copy, creative, production, media buying, suppliers and whoever holds the institutional approval all need to be working from one brief. Six people nodding in a meeting is not that.
  • Then delivery. Feedback, approvals, timelines, dependencies, quality control, and the grind of moving an approved idea into something public. I spent years behind a camera before I did any of this, which mainly helps me tell when a schedule is lying.
  • Afterwards I look at what actually happened and change the account, rather than run it again the same way.

Selected work

GIZ Egypt, CBUID

Communication and Visibility Advisor, September 2024 to February 2025

I came into this as leave cover, which meant picking up a live communications mandate halfway through instead of starting one. The programme was Capacity Building through Urban Infrastructure Development, co-funded by the EU and BMZ.

The work covered press material, social content, video, event materials and visual assets. Coordination ran across ministries, governorates, city councils, international partners and the programme teams. I worked on infrastructure inaugurations in Alexandria and Beheira, on the project's closing ceremony, and on activity around the World Urban Forum, which included pre-event planning, policy dialogue materials, video production and press support.

Donor visibility is unforgiving in a specific way. Protocol, timing, institutional accuracy and public narrative all have to be right in the same document, and none of it can be corrected once it's out.

Dlight Media Solutions

Senior Account Manager, June 2026 to present

Sixteen active accounts, all of them mine. On any given week that means taking a brief that arrived as a phone call and turning it into scope, separating two projects a client thinks are one, writing the proposal, arguing the quotation, pulling four rounds of contradictory feedback into a single direction a designer can actually use, and keeping the photographer, the media buyer and the printer pointed at the same deadline.

I also built the system holding it together. Account documentation across twenty-two workspaces covering briefs, decisions, commitments, approvals, dependencies and follow-ups, because sixteen accounts is more than anyone should be tracking from memory.

Sherlocked Egypt

Communications and creative direction, through The Digital Venture

Five branches, horror escape rooms, a young audience and a business that depends entirely on whether people book. I've directed the account for most of its public life, including a gap when it moved elsewhere and came back.

The useful shift early on was recognising that the product isn't the room. People pay for being frightened, for getting out, and for having something to tell everyone the next day. Once that was settled the rest followed: brand narrative and room-level storytelling, content across Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, photography and video, organic and paid media planning, seasonal campaigns around school term and Halloween, branch coordination, and a long look at where the booking journey was losing people.

Commercial results are held with the client and I'm happy to talk through them directly.

Background

I trained as an architect, and the training is still how I read a situation, as parts that depend on each other rather than a list.

What came after wasn't planned as a career path. Photography and video first, then editorial and documentary work for Egyptian Geographic Magazine, then three years of field research with Transport for Cairo on walkability, bus rapid transit and electric bus infrastructure across Greater Cairo. Communications and PR grew out of all of it.

The useful residue is that I've done most of the jobs I now brief other people to do. I've collected the data in the street, carried the camera, written the release, defended the scope and sat opposite the client while all of it was argued over.

The formal training came later and in pieces. Professional Public Relations at the American University in Cairo in 2019, and a diplomacy course at ARTC Egypt the year before that.
