About

I'm Mohamed Nour Elrouby. I work across digital and institutional communications, leading accounts from the first brief through strategy, production and delivery.

A few years ago I helped organise something called The Socratic Exercise at Meeting Point of Civilizations, a philosophy and community space in Cairo. The method is as old as Socrates. A group argues its way towards a position instead of trading opinions. What struck me, sitting through those sessions, was how often a long disagreement turned out to be about the wrong question.

Most of my work since has been that same job. Find the real question before making anything.

It started earlier, in architecture. Six years of drawings, where a question about one wall is never about one wall. It is about the budget, the light and the room next door. I practised afterwards in interior and furniture design, which is the same thinking at a smaller scale, where you see the consequences quickly.

Then the camera. I went freelance in 2012 and later spent two years at Innovideas, shooting for embassies and for companies like Huawei, Nokia and Unilever. A camera slows you down in a useful way. You have to look at something properly before you decide how to show it. After a while I got more interested in the subjects than the pictures, so I started doing documentary and editorial work for Egyptian Geographic Magazine.

I joined Meeting Point in 2018 and stayed seven years. That is where I learned PR properly, with NGOs, embassies, government bodies and cultural institutions, and where I ran three cycles of a youth programme called Tamkeen. It is also where I learned that changing someone's mind takes far longer than any campaign plan allows for.

In 2021 I joined Transport for Cairo as a field researcher, and questions stopped being a habit and became a method. I rode the buses. I counted traffic on main roads. I talked to more than 200 people around Greater Cairo about how they get around and what makes it hard. Standing at a junction counting cars is not glamorous, but you cannot argue with what you have counted yourself. The same year I set up The Digital Venture, a small agency that builds a team for each project instead of keeping one on the payroll.

At the end of 2024 I was at GIZ Egypt, covering the communication advisor role on an urban development programme while a colleague was on leave. The leave landed on the busiest months: the German President's visit, two infrastructure inaugurations, the World Urban Forum, and the closing ceremony, where I chose the photographs and the video that a room of ministers and EU officials would see. It was my first time inside a big international institution, and it made the same point from another direction. A press release is the last step after months of technical work, and it is worth very little if the person writing it never asked what came before.

Since June 2026 I have been Senior Account Manager at Dlight Media Solutions. I am the only account manager there, so all 16 accounts come through me. A brief usually arrives as a phone call, and my first job is still to find the question inside it.

None of this was a plan. Architecture, photography, philosophy, research and agency work felt like separate careers for years. They turned out to be one job, and what holds it together is account leadership: working out what a project actually needs, keeping the right people pointed the same way, and owning the result.
